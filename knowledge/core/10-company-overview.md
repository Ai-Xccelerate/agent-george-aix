# Onyx — Company Overview

Onyx (getonyx.ai) builds software that helps Microsoft partners — MSPs and CSPs — win, support, and grow enterprise Microsoft customers. The lead product, Transition Hub, helps a partner price and win an Enterprise Agreement to CSP transition; the second, Support Hub, gives that partner a scalable way to answer the licensing questions that arrive once the customer is on. Onyx ships software, not a service, and pairs the platform with a human program-management ("coaching") layer that teaches partners how to use it.

## Elevator

Onyx is a Microsoft-ecosystem software company that gives smaller and mid-sized Microsoft partners the muscle they have never had: a platform that runs the assessments, builds the scenarios, and answers the licensing questions that the £100 gorillas — Software One, SHI, CDW — do today with hundreds of people on payroll. The bet is that the local partner who already does the customer's telephony, security, or Office 365 work can now also win the Microsoft contract, and beat the scaled reseller at it.

## Why we exist

Microsoft has told tens of thousands of level-A and level-B EA customers that CSP is their future. Local partners want that business and have never been able to compete on the licensing-translation, pricing, and incentive complexity that scaled resellers handle with large pre-sales and consulting teams. Onyx exists so partners do not have to hire those teams to compete.

> "Microsoft has essentially communicated to tens of thousands of level A and level B customers around the world that CSP is their future." — John

> "The number one challenge that we're facing is actually showing a partner that they've got the right to win that business." — John

## Founding team

The founding leadership built the SoftwareOne North America Microsoft practice before starting Onyx. The team referenced in working sessions includes John Dusett (CEO), Chris, Neil, and Fraser. The company has subsidiaries in the Nordics and South Africa, with LATAM, Dubai, and Asia named as planned next regions.

> Internal only — do not paraphrase to customers.
> The Microsoft-practice background is the credibility hook for the partner conversation. The "ex-Software One" story is part of why partners trust the platform's licensing logic.

## How Onyx makes money

Onyx licenses the platform to the partner at a flat fee — not per assessment, not per seat of the partner's customer. The same model applies to the Arrow pilot, where Arrow funds the partners' access and the fee is co-op eligible. A typical pilot deal pays for itself out of the first big EA-to-CSP win, where the underlying customer contract is in the $700K–$1.5M range for a level-A EA. Onyx also offers an optional fractional Microsoft CRO add-on alongside the license.

> "We focus on licensing it to the partner and then letting them scale their business with it… it's a simple bring-the-platform-in license charge." — John

## Customer mix

Approximately 95% of customers are partners (MSPs / CSPs); 5% are direct end-customers. The partner is the buyer, the user, and the deployment surface — the partner's customers see the platform branded as the partner's, not as Onyx.

## Recent milestones

- Transition Hub has been in market roughly six months and is now the lead product.
- Annual recurring revenue is approaching $1M.
- Three to four customers have closed whose primary purchase was Support Hub.
- Arrow has nominated seven partners for a pilot of Transition Hub, with another five on a waiting list. Arrow funds the pilot — no charge to the partner.
- Partners running on Transition Hub today have over a 50% win rate on the proposals they generate.
- Onyx ran a 45-minute session at Ultimate Partner in Seattle (May 2026) co-presented with Arrow, covering 50–70 attendees including MSPs and other vendors.

## What's next, thematically

- **Transition Hub is the focus.** Sales Hub is parked. Support Hub is being simplified onto the surfaces customers already use — Teams, Copilot, shared inbox, email.
- **Scale the coaching layer.** Today the program-manager (coach) capacity caps how many partners can be onboarded per month. The plan is to keep the human coach in the loop while putting agent assistance underneath them, so one coach can support many more partners.
- **Hardened Transition Hub on Azure**, with capacity testing for hundreds of partners.
- **Agentic-by-design 2.0 architecture** — a shared foundation (Onyx Core analog: auth, users, KB, integrations) with Transition / Support / Sales hubs as modules on top.
- **Maya 2.0** — the in-app coach evolves from a usability helper into a multilingual, multimodal coach that lives in every app.
- **Persona-based views and an "execute" layer.** Same Transition Hub data, different lenses for partner AM, partner SE, customer CFO, customer CIO; and a button on optimizations so somebody can actually action them, not just see them.
- **Beyond Microsoft, eventually.** The framework is meant to apply to Cisco, IBM, Oracle later — explicitly not the current focus.
